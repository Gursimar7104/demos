Review and complaint triage for Booko

Booko sells dynamic pricing so unused time slots do not go to zero. Public booking-tool complaints already cluster around no-shows, cancellation rules, and hours that sit empty.

Built by Gursimar Singh, Nov 2026

The Founding Growth Lead role includes outbound, positioning, and a growth playbook. I grouped publicly described pain into themes a Booko first conversation can use.

Booko’s site says it prices time slots from forecast demand for businesses such as fitness studios, med spas, and tours, and that unsold time disappears forever. Source: booko.com and the YC company page (the YC page also states early customers see about 20% revenue uplift; that is the company’s claim, not my measurement).

ThemeWhat buyers already say in publicBooko angle
Empty inventory is lost forever Booko’s own homepage is built around this: “Make more money from every hour you sell.” Lead with unused-hour recovery, not a generic “AI pricing” headline.
Static prices on existing booking tools Booko says it plugs into the systems a studio already runs, instead of replacing the booking stack. Outbound should name the incumbent (Mindbody, Fresha, Acuity, Booker) and ask about leftover off-peak hours, not a rip-and-replace.
Spa and salon operators already buy scheduling software Booker markets itself as spa and salon management software, which is the same inventory problem Booko describes. A first target list can start with studios already paying for a scheduler but still posting last-minute openings.

Sources: booko.com, booker.com.

What I would build next

A weekly intake of public last-minute class and appointment openings from a short list of New York studios, scored by how close to start time the slot is still unsold. That is a proof the growth lead can attach to outreach without asking the studio for data first.
